What the backtest found

A pattern can recur without adding predictive value.

Across NQ, Nasdaq-100 cash, and S&P 500, all 18 primary four-week excess-return confidence intervals include zero: three concepts × two directions × three markets. These are pointwise exploratory intervals, not proof that the true effect is exactly zero.

SWEEPS

A higher return is not automatically an edge

NQ gained 1.19% on average over four weeks after a prior-week low sweep and rejection (195 observations). Its same-era baseline was 0.95%: an excess of 0.24 percentage points, with a 95% interval from −0.45 to +0.99 pp.

FAIR-VALUE GAPS

Many gaps are revisited

Within 13 weeks, NQ reached the midpoint of 79 / 114 bullish displacement FVGs (69.3%) and 40 / 49 bearish FVGs (81.6%). These denominators require complete follow-up; passing a level is not evidence of an executable fill.

HISTORICAL LIMIT

Longer history needs better evidence

The S&P archive starts in 1927, but usable high/low/close observations begin in 1962. Open-dependent tests start in 1982. NQ futures reach 2000; the older Nasdaq-100 cash record begins in 1985.

02 /

Define the concept before testing it

Market liquidity describes how easily an order can trade, including spread, depth and price impact. Weekly OHLC observes none of these directly. Here, “buy-side” and “sell-side liquidity” mean candidate stop-location proxies above a known high or below a known low. They are hypotheses about possible order locations, not observed orders. CME methodology.

A · Sweep and rejection

Use the high and low of the preceding k completed weeks. A breach alone is not a rejection. A directional sweep must close back through the breached level; a week breaching both sides is excluded.

SSL: Lₜ < Dₜ and Cₜ > Dₜ
BSL: Hₜ > Uₜ and Cₜ < Uₜ
Uₜ = max prior k highs
Dₜ = min prior k lows

Primary k = 1; sensitivity k = 4, 13, 52. Strict inequalities. No retrospectively confirmed swings.

B · Three-candle fair-value gap

An FVG is non-overlap between the outer candles of a three-candle sequence. The middle candle may have traded through the zone. The formation is known only after the third week closes.

Bullish: Lₜ > Hₜ₋₂
Bearish: Hₜ < Lₜ₋₂
CE = (zone low + zone high) / 2

It is neither a measured order-book void nor evidence that price must return. “Full fill” here means reaching the far edge.

C · Displacement and sequence

The displacement subset requires a directional middle-candle body at least half the prior 14-week average true range, with that candle spanning the whole zone.

|Cₜ₋₁ − Oₜ₋₁| ≥ 0.5 × ATR14ₜ₋₂
Body direction agrees with FVG
Prior sweep occurs in t−3 … t−1

ATR uses a simple mean of true ranges. The sequence is a mechanical weekly proxy; it does not implement the full discretionary ICT model.

Read the uncertainty correctly

Primary four-week excess-return intervals use 1,000 resamples of 26-week blocks, preserving more serial dependence than treating events as independent coin tosses. Same-era baselines are recalculated in each resample.

The interval is withheld below 20 observations and for non-primary variants. Intervals are pointwise, exploratory and not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Do not select whichever row happens to exclude zero. No causal or tradable-edge conclusion follows.

Compare markets on the same definition

Identical selected concept, direction and horizon. Cash indices, futures continuations, and stock prices have different economic meanings; no pooled average is shown.

“Excluded” includes an unfinished tail or a missing/invalid week in follow-up. These events are not counted as failures. Median time is conditional on reaching the midpoint within that horizon.

A simple mechanical check

For comparison with the event study, this separate test enters at the next weekly open, exits at the fourth following weekly close, and permits one position at a time per market and rule. It has no stop, target, leverage, or optimized parameter.

Gross, 10 bp and 25 bp are illustrative round-trip cost scenarios. Results are signed price changes, not an investable equity curve. No dividends in the base stock variant; adjusted variants use consistently adjusted OHLC. Futures roll P&L, financing, actual commissions and stock-borrow constraints are not modeled. Cash indices cannot be traded directly. Profit factor = positive net price returns / absolute negative net price returns.

S&P next-open tests are disabled before 26 April 1982. A weekly high/low range does not resolve stop/target ordering; the fixed-horizon model avoids assuming such an order. This simplified model is an illustration, not a complete ICT trading strategy.

How far back can the numbers support?

Oldest obtained usable pattern history

Bars show the span between eligible weeks, not uninterrupted coverage. Interior gaps remain gaps. Counts and raw-history boundaries below are authoritative.

The S&P boundary matters

The downloaded series begins 30 December 1927. Before 1962, its daily open, high, low and close repeat a single closing value. Weekly extrema built from those closes would omit intraday highs and lows, so they are excluded.

From 1962 to April 1982, approximately 94.3% of opens equal the previous close. This report uses that era only for HLC-dependent sweeps, raw FVGs and close-reference outcomes; displacement and next-open tests require later OHLC.

The S&P 500 itself launched in 1957. Pre-launch back-history does not mean a 500-stock index was trading in 1927. This study does not manufacture older weekly OHLC from monthly prices.

NQ source and coverage boundaries

The read-only local database contains 4,807,402 one-minute candles from 6 June 2010 22:00 UTC through 4 September 2026 20:59 UTC. Early years are sparse.

The local check requires 6,000 observed minutes per week; some holiday weeks also fail this conservative screen. The fixed historical segment ends 11 August 2026 22:49 UTC; its last whole research week is 7 August. Mutable or mixed weeks appear only in the separate snapshot variant.

Yahoo NQ=F is a separate continuation starting in September 2000. No source splicing, common-contract assumption, or use of NDX as pre-2000 NQ futures.

Method, limitations and evidence

01

Freeze the rules

Coverage inspected first. Definitions and outcomes written before event returns were calculated. No performance-driven tuning of lookbacks or the 0.5 ATR threshold.

02

Preserve the inputs

Raw vendor JSON and SHA-256 hashes retained. Local market storage read-only. Weekly grids preserve gaps; no interpolation or source splicing.

03

Separate the questions

Close-reference event returns, FVG threshold reaches, and next-open mechanical checks have separate denominators and meanings.

04

Verify the implementation

Boundary fixtures, future-data invariance, independent event calculations, source aggregation checks, and browser interaction checks accompany the artifact.

What these results cannot establish

Actual resting stops, institutional intent, intraday price path, stop-loss fills, market impact, or a full ICT trading model. The same-era baseline controls for coarse historical drift, not volatility, regime, trend, or causality.

Surviving stocks omit delisted failures and introduce selection bias. Corporate actions, stale quotes, thin trading, and different currencies prevent a naive pooled result. All returns remain in each instrument’s own price units.

A 70/30 chronological split is a robustness check, not a separately registered untouched holdout. Overlapping event returns remain dependent. The 26-week bootstrap reduces, but does not eliminate, dependence concerns.

Known data gaps

No authenticated pre-September-2000 NQ OHLC was obtained; Nasdaq-100 data from its January 1985 launch to September 1985 is absent. S&P pre-1962 high/low evidence is unusable. Company histories far predate accessible daily data.

Daily data is not independently certified against every exchange holiday or original tick. Uniform 10/25 bp costs do not validate historical executions. NQ contract rolls and stock distributions have not been reconstructed from original corporate-action or contract records.

The oldest data obtained and screened is reported, not an absolute limit on what licensed or archival research might recover. Stooq downloads returned verification pages; Yale’s longer archival prices do not establish the required OHLC.
